<h3 class="era-heading">Why this section exists</h3>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;">Every other subject here leans on linear algebra and calculus it never teaches. The <a href="{{ '/vision/multi-view-geometry/' | relative_url }}">multi-view geometry</a> guide factorises an essential matrix, the <a href="{{ '/vision/nonlinear-optimization/' | relative_url }}">nonlinear optimization</a> guide solves normal equations and takes Newton steps, and the <a href="{{ '/ai/llm-training/' | relative_url }}">LLM guides</a> are matrix multiplies and gradients all the way down. This section is where that machinery gets built properly &mdash; geometry first, algebra second, every idea attached to something you can drag.</p>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;"><strong>Linear Algebra, Interactively</strong> starts at what a vector is and ends at attention matrices, Gauss-Newton and SO(3). <strong>Calculus, Interactively</strong> is single-variable calculus first, carried by a robot moving along a line, then multivariable calculus, carried by a two-link arm whose Jacobian <em>is</em> the derivative as a matrix; <strong>Calculus in Motion</strong> lets that arm move &mdash; vector fields, constrained optimization, matrix calculus and backprop, differential equations, and calculus on manifolds. Neither calculus volume re-teaches gradient descent, Newton's method or the matrix exponential as algorithms &mdash; those live in Nonlinear Optimization, which these parts feed by name. Every part in every series ends with a "where this shows up" line pointing at the page elsewhere on the site that uses it.</p>
