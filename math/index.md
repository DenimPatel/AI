<h3 class="era-heading">How to read the two volumes</h3>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;">Volume I is single-variable calculus first, carried by a robot moving along a line - position, velocity and acceleration are the derivative twice, and distance travelled is the integral of speed - and then multivariable calculus, carried by a two-link arm whose forward kinematics is a composition, whose reachable set is a surface, and whose Jacobian <em>is</em> the derivative as a matrix. Volume II lets that arm move: vector fields, line and surface integrals, the Jacobian determinant, constrained optimisation, matrix calculus and backprop, optimizers, probability, differential equations, calculus on manifolds, and the calculus of variations.</p>
<p class="section-lede" style="max-width:68ch;">Neither volume re-teaches gradient descent, Newton's method or the matrix exponential as algorithms - those live in <a href="{{ '/vision/nonlinear-optimization/' | relative_url }}">Nonlinear Optimization</a>, which these parts feed by name. Every part ends with a "where this shows up" line pointing at the page elsewhere on the site that uses it.</p>
